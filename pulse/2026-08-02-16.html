States Are Repricing the AI Data Center Bargain

The Information

The Information reports that four U.S. states have rolled back or paused data center tax incentives, while nine more are considering repeal. The direct cost impact is large: if a gigawatt-scale AI campus carries roughly $40 billion of IT equipment, a 7% sales tax can add nearly $3 billion per gigawatt before land, buildings, power, and grid upgrades.

Texas is the big swing factor. Gov. Greg Abbott has asked the legislature to revisit data center sales tax exemptions when it reconvenes in 2027, even as the state is expected to become the largest data center market by power capacity by 2030. Virginia took a compromise route with a new electricity consumption tax; Louisiana is pushing data center companies to fully fund their electricity needs if they want incentives.

My take: this is the next layer of AI margin pressure. The market talks about GPU supply and model efficiency, but the real unit economics increasingly depend on state-level politics, grid cost allocation, and whether communities believe the projects pay their way.

"If those go away or are yanked all of a sudden, your entire business plan is in flux."

Why it matters: For Hiro and AI product strategy, this is a reminder that model costs are not just chips and inference curves. Infrastructure policy can move the frontier price of intelligence by billions at a time.
