Zimbabwe, SpaceX, and the Trouble With Measuring Wealth in Dollars

Josh Blanchfield from Avos Capital

Josh Blanchfield uses the fictional-but-plausible-feeling spectacle of a SpaceX IPO and Elon Musk becoming a trillionaire to make a sharper point: nominal dollar figures are a bad measuring stick when the dollar itself is melting against goods, services, education, health insurance, and financial assets.

The useful move is his insistence on a third reference point. Looking at USD versus other currencies makes the dollar look relatively stable; looking at USD versus bread, college, health insurance, Exxon, or GDP tells a more revealing story. His Rockefeller comparison lands the point: the richest person at roughly 3% of U.S. GDP may be a more stable frame than the headline number of billionaire or trillionaire.

"Fiat money is melting in front of our eyes. Sorry Elon, but a trillion bucks isn't worth what it used to be."

Why it matters: Good framing for Hiro: wealth, returns, and financial advice depend heavily on the unit of account. Users think in dollars, but real planning needs purchasing power and relative claims on the economy.
