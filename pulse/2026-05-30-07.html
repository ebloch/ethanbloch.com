Anthropic's Numbers Are Getting Too Big to Trust Without an S-1

On my Om

Om Malik's sharpest point is not that Anthropic is overvalued; it is that the underlying revenue number is becoming impossible to audit from the outside. The company is reportedly around a $30B to $40B annualized run rate, but Om is asking the right question: how much is net revenue, durable revenue, cash versus credits, and related-party cloud circularity?

For Ethan, this is the AI valuation debate in its most useful form. Multiples are almost secondary when the base is press-release ARR inside a supply chain where hyperscalers invest in labs, labs commit cloud spend back, and everyone needs the top line to look inevitable.

My take: this is why consumer/product traction and unit economics matter more than lab mystique. The next AI finance story is going to be accounting, not benchmarks.

"Run rate is a press-release number."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to how to think about OpenAI/Anthropic-style AI economics, and a useful caution for any AI-native finance product built around opaque model-infrastructure markets.

Wall Street Copies Crypto's Political Machine

FinTech Collective

FinTech Collective frames the bank lobby's new strategy as regulatory market-neutrality: fund groups on the left, fund groups on the right, and win either way. Crypto's Fairshake PAC showed the model works, spending $135M in 2024 and landing allies on committees that write crypto law; now the eight largest U.S. banks have reportedly pushed about $100M into the American Growth Alliance.

The immediate fight is stablecoin yield, but the broader lesson is that fintech regulation is increasingly shaped by organized political infrastructure, not just policy arguments. Incumbents are copying the playbook of the firms they used to dismiss.

My take: this is a reminder that the financial product surface is only half the battlefield. Distribution, trust, and regulatory posture are part of the product.

"Wall Street has decided the responsible move is to behave exactly like the upstarts it spent years lecturing."

Why it matters: For Hiro, policy and incumbent-bank incentives matter because personal finance products live inside regulated rails. This is the political-economy layer behind stablecoins, banking access, and yield products.

Agents Are Becoming Managed Execution Environments

AINews

AINews' most useful synthesis today is that Google, OpenAI, Cursor, and others are converging on vertically integrated agent stacks: model, harness, sandbox, UI, remote control, quotas, and memory. Google's Managed Agents provision sandboxed Linux environments from the Gemini API; Spark pushes the consumer side; OpenAI's Codex expands Windows computer use and mobile remote steering.

This is the shift from chatbot to operator. The interesting moat is less raw model quality and more the full control plane around long-running work: traces, cost controls, permissions, observable execution, and recovery.

My take: for finance agents, this is exactly the shape that will matter. Money movement and advice need constrained execution environments, not just better conversations.

"The common pattern is less chatbot, more managed execution environment with policy and memory."

Why it matters: Maps directly onto Hiro's thesis: trusted financial agents need tools, permissions, memory, auditability, and bounded action spaces.
