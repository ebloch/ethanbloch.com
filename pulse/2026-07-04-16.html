California's November Ballot Is Already a Policy Thicket

GrowSF

GrowSF flags 14 statewide measures headed for the November 2026 ballot, spanning housing bonds, fiscal reserves, high-earner tax rates, public campaign financing, recall rules, voter ID, wealth taxes, special-tax thresholds, clinic spending rules, and CEQA streamlining.

The interesting pattern is how much of California governance is now fought through ballot-box fiscal architecture: bonds, reserve formulas, tax constraints, initiative countermeasures, and CEQA carveouts. For anyone thinking about housing, public finance, or civic capacity, the ballot is less a list of discrete issues than a map of where the legislature cannot settle conflicts cleanly.

My take: Prop 45 is the one to watch beyond the obvious tax fights. If voters put broader project streamlining into law, that could harden the state's shift away from process maximalism and toward permitting throughput.

"We’ve got our work cut out for us!"

Why it matters: Useful local operating context for Ethan: housing, taxes, CEQA, and state fiscal capacity all shape the Bay Area environment Hiro is being built in.
