Google's SpaceX Compute Deal Looks Like an Nvidia Signal

Stratechery

Ben Thompson reads Google's reported $920 million-per-month SpaceX compute contract as more than a one-off capacity lease. The useful inference is that demand for Google Cloud AI may be less about generic cloud or TPUs and more about customers wanting access to Nvidia chips specifically.

That matters because the same issue pairs the SpaceX deal with Broadcom's AI-chip disappointment. If Google's TPU ecosystem is effectively a small-customer-base business while Nvidia remains the common denominator for everyone else, the AI infrastructure market is still consolidating around Nvidia even when the buyer is Google.

My take: for Hiro, this is a reminder that agent products are still riding very physical constraints. UX may feel software-native, but margins, latency, and reliability are being set by who controls scarce compute.

"Perhaps the answer is that everyone-but-Anthropic wants Nvidia chips, not TPUs?"

Why it matters: Infrastructure scarcity shapes agent economics. If even Google is buying bridge Nvidia capacity, compute cost and latency should stay central in product and business planning.

OpenAI Public-Stake Talks Put AI Wealth Sharing on the Table

TLDR AI

OpenAI and the Trump administration reportedly discussed a possible government stake in the company through donated equity, connected to a broader Public Wealth Fund idea that would let citizens share in AI-driven upside. The Information's AI Agenda adds that Trump has been talking about financial partnerships with AI companies and framed the public as potentially becoming a partner.

This is a striking policy turn: the conversation is moving from regulating AI externalities to explicitly negotiating who owns the gains. It also sits awkwardly beside frontier-lab safety debates, because a government equity stake could make the state both referee and beneficiary.

My take: this is early and politically messy, but the direction matters. If AI becomes central enough to productivity, governments will not be content with tax receipts alone.

"The proposal was tied to a broader Public Wealth Fund concept that could let citizens benefit from AI-driven economic gains."

Why it matters: Ethan is close to both AI deployment and consumer finance. Sovereign participation in AI upside would reshape incentives around ownership, regulation, and distribution.

Ramp Stack Pushes Spend Management Into the Accounting OS

TLDR Fintech

Ramp launched Ramp Stack, an AI accounting operating system meant to handle month-end close, cash reconciliation, transaction coding, and journal entries with reviewable, auditable decisions. The product arrived alongside a $750 million equity raise valuing Ramp at $44 billion.

The interesting part is the direction of travel: finance automation is moving from card/spend workflows into the system-of-record layer where accounting, auditability, and reconciliation live. Airwallex's Leapfin acquisition in the same issue points the same way.

My take: fintech platforms are racing toward the CFO back office because transaction data plus workflow ownership is a better AI wedge than a standalone chatbot.

"Ramp launched Ramp Stack, an AI accounting operating system designed to handle workflows like month-end close, cash reconciliation, transaction coding, and journal entries with reviewable, auditable decisions."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro's thesis: proprietary financial data plus trusted workflow execution is where AI can become a product, not a feature.
