San Francisco Built Only 405 Homes in the First Half of 2026

GrowSF

San Francisco completed just 405 new homes in the first six months of 2026, while state housing targets require more than 82,000 homes by 2031. The pipeline looks large on paper, but only 3,301 proposed homes have reached construction, which is the part that actually matters.

The deeper problem is not just permitting delay. GrowSF points to the Controller's finding that nearly every housing type is financially infeasible under current rules and market conditions, even after recent fee cuts. My take: this is the useful frame for local politics now: approval reform matters, but the binding constraint is increasingly whether approved projects pencil at all.

"After a decade whittling away at the bureaucratic barriers to housing, few remain truly insurmountable. But as uncertainty has declined, costs have risen, leaving even approved projects unable to break ground."

Why it matters: Ethan lives in the Bay Area and cares about systems that work. This is a clear example of policy, capital markets, and local execution all colliding: the city can be technically pro-housing and still fail to produce homes.
