Congress Reopens the Earned Wage Access Fight

Fintech Business Weekly

H.R. 9330, the Earned Wage Access Consumer Protection Act, passed out of the House Financial Services Committee 29-22. The bill would require no-cost access options, fee and tip disclosures, dispute processes, overdraft reimbursement when providers debit incorrectly, and bans on repayment collection suits, credit card repayment, late fees, and credit bureau furnishing.

The real fight is preemption. The bill would block states from treating compliant EWA as credit or substantially restricting compliant products, which is exactly what providers want after a patchwork of at least 12 state laws. Consumer groups oppose it because EWA can still look economically like high-cost credit, especially when tips and expedited funding fees are annualized.

My take: this is the kind of regulatory boundary-setting that matters for consumer finance product design. The strongest version would separate employer-integrated EWA from direct-to-consumer models, because the data quality and repayment risk are structurally different.

"earned wage access may be the "least bad" option"

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro's world: short-term liquidity, disclosure design, credit classification, and how regulators draw lines around products that do not fit old lending categories.
