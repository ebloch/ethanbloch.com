Index Rebalancing Has Become a Pod-Shop Profit Center

Bloomberg Money Stuff

Matt Levine uses SpaceX's Nasdaq-100 inclusion to explain how the market has mostly solved the old index-addition squeeze: passive funds need to buy at the close, while liquidity providers and pod shops pre-position around that demand. The smoother close is good market plumbing, but the economic rent has not disappeared; it has moved to funds that can forecast and warehouse the flow.

The striking data point is Millennium index-rebalance teams reportedly making about $3.7 billion in June, more than half the firm's pre-fee profit for the month. My take: this is a reminder that 'passive' investing still creates active microstructure problems, and the edge accrues to whoever can model forced buyers best.

"The hedge funds provide good customer service."

Why it matters: For Hiro, this is a useful example of hidden costs and incentives inside seemingly simple financial products. It is not enough to know the ETF fee; flows, timing, and market structure matter too.
