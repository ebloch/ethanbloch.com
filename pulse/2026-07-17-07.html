Kimi K3 Turns the Open-Model Debate Back On

Platformer

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8T-parameter multimodal model aimed at coding, research, and long-running agent tasks, with weights promised by July 27. It topped Arena for front-end design and sparked a mini DeepSeek-style market and policy debate, but the better read is more nuanced: K3 looks genuinely strong, especially for open weights, while still trailing the best closed models in usability and reliability.

The important part for Ethan is not the leaderboard victory by itself. It is the stack pressure: models that are frontier-ish, cheaper, and eventually portable will force product teams to win on harnesses, memory, integrations, workflows, and distribution. For Hiro, that reinforces the thesis that durable value sits in financial context and trusted execution, not just access to the smartest model API.

My take: treat this as a product-infrastructure warning, not a panic. If model capability keeps compressing, the moat shifts even harder toward proprietary workflow loops, user trust, and cost-aware orchestration.

"Very good model, not Sol Max or Fable, but great open weights."

Why it matters: Hiro will live in exactly this world: multiple capable models, uneven reliability, and real economic pressure to route tasks intelligently instead of worshiping one frontier provider.

Public Software Is Being Repriced Around AI Durability

a16z

a16z argues the software sell-off is selective, not indiscriminate. Multiples on next-twelve-month free cash flow are back near 2014 levels, but investors are separating companies with perceived AI defensibility from horizontal SaaS and generic point solutions. Cybersecurity, observability, and vertical SaaS are getting more benefit of the doubt because they have trust, workflow depth, and specialized data.

That framing matters for Hiro because personal finance is closer to vertical workflow software than generic productivity software. The question is whether the product owns enough trusted context and repeated user behavior to become a system of record, not just a prettier chat layer on top of financial accounts.

My take: markets are starting to ask the right question. Software revenue is not enough; the question is whether AI makes the product more central or makes it easier to replace.

"software alone is not a moat."

Why it matters: A clean mental model for personal finance AI: durable advantage will come from trust, workflow ownership, and data/context loops, not generic conversational UI.
