GrowSF Pushes Voters Before a Low-Turnout June 2 Primary

GrowSF

GrowSF's core message is turnout: with three days left before the June 2 San Francisco primary, only about 70,000 ballots had been cast, and they estimate the final total will land around 200,000 to 220,000 voters. The edition doubles as a voter-guide push, listing endorsements across city, state, federal, and ballot-measure races.

The local policy signal is broader than the ballot itself. GrowSF is tying its endorsements to a pro-growth city agenda: more market-rate housing, clearer rental pricing, operational competence in City Hall, and dedicated transit funding.

My take: this is the kind of local politics that matters even if you are not steeped in SF City Hall. The people who show up in low-turnout primaries choose the operating system for housing, transportation, schools, and public finance.

"So if you’re one of the ~150,000 people who haven’t voted yet, now is the time!"

Why it matters: Useful civic context for Ethan's Bay Area life: local policy choices around housing, taxes, transit, and governance are being shaped by a relatively small electorate.
