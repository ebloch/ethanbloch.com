The Knicks Might Actually Be Great

Silver Bulletin

Nate Silver argues that New York's Finals run should not be dismissed as a fluke. The core point is statistical: playoff performance in the NBA is meaningfully informative because the sport generates so many possessions, and the Knicks' current run is extreme even after adjusting for likely shooting luck.

The more interesting angle is that this is not the usual young-team-breakout or veteran-coasted-through-the-regular-season story. Silver sees a team whose defense improved materially over the season, whose playoff experience now matters, and whose offense has become less Brunson-dependent because Karl-Anthony Towns is functioning more like a second creator.

My take: the broader lesson is about stale priors. A team, company, or product can look capped until one latent variable changes: role clarity, operating rhythm, or one key integration. Then the same ingredients suddenly compound differently.

"I think the Knicks could actually win the NBA title"

Why it matters: Useful sports-cultural context for Ethan, and a nice analytical case study in when recent evidence should override an older model.
