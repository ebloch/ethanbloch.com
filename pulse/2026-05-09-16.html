Stablecoins Are Outgrowing the Name

a16z crypto

Robert Hackett argues that "stablecoin" is becoming a skeuomorphic term: useful when the main problem was crypto volatility, increasingly misleading now that the category is turning into payment and application infrastructure. The important shift is from an asset that merely holds value to money that can move instantly, settle globally, and live inside software workflows.

The framing matters because naming affects mental models. If people keep hearing "stablecoin" as a defensive crypto patch, they miss the product surface: programmable dollars, embedded financial flows, and internet-native settlement. My take: for consumer finance, the interesting question is less whether users knowingly adopt stablecoins and more when they quietly become the rail under better money movement experiences.

"Stability is now table stakes. It's a prerequisite, and not the point."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro/OpenAI finance: stablecoins are less a crypto asset class than a possible substrate for faster transfers, embedded savings, and agent-executed financial workflows.

GrowSF Says Prop D Is Really a Gross Receipts Tax Hike

GrowSF

GrowSF's case against San Francisco Prop D is that the "CEO tax" branding hides the mechanism: it raises a gross receipts tax starting in 2027, which means it taxes sales attributed to SF rather than profits. That is especially punishing for low-margin retailers, groceries, and pharmacies, where costs are more likely to show up as higher prices.

The broader point is institutional, not just partisan. SF has repeatedly changed business tax rules by ballot measure, which makes planning harder and amplifies the city's reputation for unstable operating conditions. My take: the mechanism critique is strong even if one likes redistribution; taxing revenue instead of profit is a blunt instrument, and ballot-box tax design rarely handles edge cases well.

"This so-called "CEO tax" doesn't actually tax CEOs - it taxes your groceries."

Why it matters: Useful local policy signal for Ethan: it connects city politics, household costs, business formation, and the incentive design problems that show up in financial product work too.
