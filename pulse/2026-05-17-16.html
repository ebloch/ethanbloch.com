Signal, Noise, and the Investment Case for Staying Anchored

Josh Blanchfield from Avos Capital

Josh Blanchfield uses a week on a domestic violence jury as a lens for a much bigger point: younger jurors raised inside feeds, memes, AI fakery, and reflexive skepticism struggled to distinguish core evidence from random distractions. His investment translation is that markets are also voting machines in the short run, and many voters now process reality through the same noisy channels.

The portfolio takeaway is practical rather than apocalyptic: hold onto durable anchors when the news cycle gets performative. Blanchfield's anchors are a world moving away from globalization and disinflation, rising demand for physical infrastructure, a preference for ownership of businesses and real assets over nominal lending, liquidity in the face of AI uncertainty, and global diversification as the dollar becomes a geopolitical vulnerability.

My take: this is useful for Ethan because it connects two things that are usually discussed separately: epistemology and portfolio construction. In a world where trust in evidence degrades, the edge is not having the hottest take; it is having a small set of beliefs sturdy enough to survive the feed.

"Our species evolved to trust our senses to process reality. That's not good enough anymore."

Why it matters: Relevant to Hiro's advisory thesis: financial advice has to help people separate durable signal from persuasive noise, especially when markets, media, and AI all amplify confusion.
