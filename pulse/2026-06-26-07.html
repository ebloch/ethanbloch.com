Internal Codex Usage Is Starting to Look Like Real Organizational Adoption

AINews

AINews pulls out the most interesting signal from OpenAI Economic Research: internal Codex output tokens have exploded across non-engineering groups, with Research up 56x since November 2025, Customer Support up 32x, Engineering up 27x, and Legal up 13x. The important part is not token volume by itself; it is that adoption deepens once teams have workflows, review loops, and concrete jobs where the model can keep compounding usefulness.

My take: this is closer to the Hiro adoption problem than a normal product usage metric. Financial advice agents will not win because users send one clever prompt; they win when the agent is embedded into recurring money decisions, audit trails, support workflows, and escalation paths. The model quality matters, but so does the operating environment around it.

"Sometimes, you just have to let them cook"

Why it matters: Useful benchmark for how agent products move from novelty to default workflow inside real organizations.

The AI Trade Is Splintering Into Winners, Losers, and Cost Bearers

Bloomberg / TLDR

Bloomberg argues the market is no longer treating every AI-exposed stock as the same bet. Memory names are up more than 200% this year, data-center and electrical-equipment baskets are up more than 100%, AI semis are still strong, but the Magnificent Seven bundle is down for 2026 and software most exposed to AI disruption has lost about a third of its value. TLDR's linked State of the AI Economy piece adds the demand-side question: AI sales are now enormous, but investors still have to ask who captures revenue as token prices fall and quality rises.

My take: the market is moving from theme-buying to unit-economics scrutiny. That is healthy for fintech AI too. The durable companies will be the ones that convert model capability into specific financial outcomes, not the ones that can merely say they use AI.

"Merely owning AI no longer tells you what you own."

Why it matters: Frames the strategic question for Hiro: where does AI create durable value, and who actually keeps the margin?

Tech Rotates From Bits to Atoms, and Robotics Becomes the Private-Market Signal

a16z

a16z's chart essay says the post-pandemic cycle has inverted the post-GFC winners: capital-heavy sectors now lead, hardware is the standout inside tech, and software has followed the broader inversion. In private markets, the signal is even sharper: robotics and physical AI have moved from absent in 2016 to the second-largest top-100 private-company category, eclipsing fintech and payments, with roughly $16B invested in Q1 across just under 500 deals.

My take: the interesting part is not a simple software-is-over story. It is that AI is expanding the surface area of what software can act on. In finance, the analogous opportunity is not physical robotics, but extending AI from advice text into actual monitored workflows, account actions, and institutional-grade operations.

"It’s a rotation from bits to atoms."

Why it matters: A reminder that the next wave of AI value may come from coupling models to real-world systems, not just better chat UX.
