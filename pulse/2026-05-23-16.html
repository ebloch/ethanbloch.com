Trump's Texas Senate Endorsement Turns a Safe-ish Seat Into a Real Risk

Silver Bulletin

Nate Silver argues that Trump's late endorsement of Ken Paxton in the Texas Senate runoff may be a strategically bad move for Republicans: Paxton is likely stronger in the primary, but his scandal profile could make Texas competitive in a year when Democrats need several Senate pickups.

The interesting part is not simply that Texas might be closer than usual. It is the mechanism: Trump waited long enough to let Republicans burn tens of millions in an intraparty fight, then backed the candidate who may be easier to beat in November. Silver's rough framing is that Texas would normally be around R+4 under generic candidates, but a major scandal penalty could plausibly move it toward toss-up territory.

My take: this is a useful reminder that political power can optimize for loyalty in one market and destroy value in another. The primary electorate and the general electorate are different customer segments, and the endorsement product that wins one can poison the other.

"Putting Texas in play would make for a lot more winning combinations."

Why it matters: Good strategic read on incentives, candidate quality, and how a dominant actor can still make poor portfolio-level decisions.
