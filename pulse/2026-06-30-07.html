The CFPB May Turn Open Banking Into a Metered Right

Fintech Takes

Alex Johnson flags the weird compromise emerging in the open-banking fight: consumers can share their bank and card data for free, but fintechs may pay after some still-undetermined number of pulls. That preserves the idea of consumer-permissioned access while giving banks a pricing lever once usage crosses a cap.

The important bit is not just the fee. It is the ambiguity over who sets the threshold, how fees are determined, and whether high-frequency use cases become structurally disadvantaged. For a financial advisor product that depends on reliable account connectivity, rationing turns data access from a binary compliance question into a unit-economics and product-design problem.

My take: this is exactly where infrastructure strategy becomes product strategy. If access is metered, the winners will be the teams that can cache intelligently, explain refresh frequency to users, and avoid building experiences that require gratuitous real-time pulls.

"The proposal would essentially set up a rationing system for customer data and leaves many questions unanswered."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro: account aggregation is not just plumbing if policy starts shaping the marginal cost and reliability of every user data refresh.

AI Is Making the Solo Operator More Economically Dangerous

Derek Thompson

Derek Thompson argues for a middle path between AI doom and AI denial: the near-term labor-market signal is less mass job destruction and more leverage for small teams and solo businesses. His concrete example is a former Amazon employee using ChatGPT to navigate restaurant strategy, local compliance, inventory, customer trends, and then spin up an AI business consultancy.

The broader point is that AI is collapsing the administrative and execution overhead around entrepreneurship. If software, research, compliance navigation, and operations can be partially delegated, the minimum viable company gets smaller and more ambitious.

My take: this is one of the strongest consumer-AI use cases because it is not about replacing a single job. It is about making a person capable of coordinating a much larger surface area of work than before.

"There has never been a better or easier time to work independently. And there has never been an easier time to become a millionaire by working for yourself."

Why it matters: This reinforces the market thesis behind AI financial advice: if more people become one-person businesses, they will need better personal finance, tax, cash-flow, and planning tools.
