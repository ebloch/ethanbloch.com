Prop D Is Becoming a Test of Whether SF Voters Buy the 'CEO Tax' Frame

GrowSF

The Chronicle urged voters to reject Prop D, arguing the measure is marketed as a tax on overpaid CEOs but would actually raise San Francisco's gross-receipts tax on businesses, with costs likely passed through to consumers, workers, or lower-margin employers. GrowSF's read is that the policy mechanism matters more than the campaign frame: the revenue would go to the general fund, not a dedicated healthcare lockbox.

The useful signal for Ethan is the political-economic design problem. A tax can sound targeted while the incidence lands elsewhere, and that gap between story and mechanism is exactly where bad policy hides. My take: this is also a reminder that consumer-facing financial products should explain who actually pays, not just who appears to be charged.

"Prop D is not a CEO tax."

Why it matters: Useful local example of tax incidence, voter framing, and how financial mechanics get obscured by political messaging.
