Applied Intuition Launches Dana, an Agent Layer for Physical AI

a16z

Applied Intuition is launching Dana, an agentic platform for developing physical AI applications. The interesting part is not just robotics; it is the company’s path from unglamorous simulation tooling to the environment where industrial customers test, validate, and deploy intelligence into machines.

That is the same pattern that keeps showing up in agent businesses: own the workflow, own the evaluation loop, and the tool slowly becomes the system of record. Dana matters because physical-world AI has a burden that chat apps do not: traceability, safety cases, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and regulatory explainability.

My take: this is a clean example of why vertical agents need infrastructure depth. In finance, the equivalent is not just a nicer chat interface; it is the ledger, policy, compliance, simulation, approval, and execution environment underneath it.

"This is why tool companies can become platform companies."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro's product thesis: agentic UX becomes durable when it sits on top of domain-specific workflow, evaluation, and execution infrastructure.

Anthropic Has Looked at Robotics as the Next Intelligence Frontier

The Information

The Information reports that Anthropic and Physical Intelligence had early acquisition talks this spring, even though Physical Intelligence later denied that any current deal was happening. The strategic logic is straightforward: if frontier labs believe recursive AI improvement spills into the physical world, robotics becomes both a capability frontier and a constraint-removal strategy.

The piece also ties robotics to clinical trials, lab automation, and data center construction. That framing is more interesting than humanoid demos: embodied AI is not only about robots as products, but about accelerating the production system around AI itself.

My take: the frontier model labs are looking for the next closed loop. Coding gave them fast feedback and revenue; robotics and biology offer much messier but potentially larger feedback loops.

"Embodied intelligence (i.e., robotics) might quickly follow recursive intelligence, and follow a similar path of increasing returns at decreasing cost."

Why it matters: A useful signal on where frontier labs may push after coding agents: domains where AI can improve the physical substrate and data generation process.

Customer Service AI Is Exposing the Latency Tax

The Information

NiCE data cited by The Information says Anthropic’s Opus models are accurate but often too slow and expensive for customer service voice and chat. Opus 4.7 reportedly took more than four seconds to first token for analyzed customers, versus about one second for Gemini 3 Flash; Anthropic’s faster Opus 4.6 Flash was still nearly 100x the cost of Gemini 3 Flash.

The lesson is blunt: model quality is not a scalar. Voice support optimizes for first-token latency, interruption handling, cost, and good-enough accuracy; coding optimizes for longer-horizon reasoning. Those are different markets, even when vendors sell them as one platform.

My take: this is a good reminder that agent products should route by job, not by model brand. The orchestration layer is a product surface, not plumbing.

"My bot's going to sound really crappy."

Why it matters: Very relevant to financial advice agents: customer trust depends on responsiveness and reliability as much as raw reasoning quality.
