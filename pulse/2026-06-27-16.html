California Voters Will Revisit the Two-Thirds Rule for Local Taxes

GrowSF

California voters will decide ACA 22 in November, which would restore a two-thirds approval requirement for local special taxes placed on the ballot by citizen initiative. The measure is a compromise after a more aggressive Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association initiative threatened to unwind local tax structures and hit San Francisco funding streams directly.

The practical point: this is a governance tradeoff, not just a tax fight. A simple-majority path makes it easier to fund specific programs; a two-thirds rule makes cities prove broader consensus before adding earmarked taxes. For San Francisco, where fiscal politics already reward complicated workarounds, the details matter.

My take: the compromise sounds less destabilizing than the original HJTA measure, but it also raises the bar for city leaders to make taxes legible and defensible. That's healthy if it forces clearer priorities; painful if it just pushes funding into more opaque mechanisms.

"Passing local taxes may get a bit harder this November, with a return to pre-2020 rules."

Why it matters: Useful California context for Ethan: local finance, governance design, and how ballot mechanics shape public-service funding.

England's World Cup Question Is Talent vs. System

Silver Bulletin

Silver Bulletin uses England's 2026 World Cup run to revisit why the country's Golden Generation underperformed: plenty of talent, but muddled roles, tactical rigidity, and a reluctance to bench famous names for better fit. The current Thomas Tuchel side looks more disciplined, with clearer midfield division of labor and a willingness to omit stars who do not suit the system.

The broader insight is organizational: raw talent is not enough when the structure makes talented people collide. England's better chance this year comes from team architecture, not just a stronger roster.

My take: this is the same lesson as high-functioning product teams and agent systems. You do not win by maximizing local capability everywhere; you win by designing roles, interfaces, and constraints that let the whole system produce more than the sum of its pieces.

"A lack of talent has never been the real issue for England."

Why it matters: A sports story that doubles as a useful operating metaphor: role clarity beats star-stacking.
