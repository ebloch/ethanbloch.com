SF Congressional Race Turns a Parody Bot Into a Campaign Scandal

GrowSF

Scott Wiener's campaign launched ConnieChan.ai, a parody chatbot aimed at portraying Supervisor Connie Chan as reflexively anti-housing, anti-transit, and anti-growth. Chan's side then repeatedly prompted the bot around identity until it produced a line about her accent, which Pelosi and other Chan endorsers framed as racist and sexist.

The AI angle is the interesting part: political campaigns are now using low-stakes agents as persuasion tools, and opponents are learning to adversarially prompt them for liabilities. This is a small local example of a bigger pattern: once AI becomes an interface, the prompt transcript becomes opposition research.

My take: the bot was a gimmick, but the adversarial-prompting playbook matters. Anyone shipping public-facing AI in regulated or reputation-sensitive domains should assume hostile users will try to manufacture the worst possible output and then litigate the screenshot.

"Voters care about housing costs, safe streets, reliable transit, good schools, and whether San Francisco can function."

Why it matters: Useful local-politics context, but also a practical reminder for Hiro: public AI surfaces need abuse-aware evaluation, logging, and response policies before critics define the product by its worst prompted edge case.
