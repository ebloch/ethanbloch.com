Natural Gas Is Becoming the AI Infrastructure Constraint Everyone Can Argue About

Doomberg

Doomberg takes on Matthew Smith's thesis that AI data centers, LNG exports, and new power plants could create a US natural gas shortage by 2028-2030. The piece agrees with the demand setup and with Smith's forecast that US production can still climb from roughly 112 Bcf/day to 132 Bcf/day by late 2030, but disputes the idea that LNG exports would remain politically untouchable if domestic prices spiked to crisis levels.

The useful frame for Hiro is not just energy as a macro input. It is that AI infrastructure has moved from chips and data centers into commodity politics, permitting, and contract law. My take: the market can underestimate how quickly policy becomes the load balancer when AI demand collides with household energy prices.

"Will natural gas be the bridging fuel that carries the AI boom along until a full nuclear renaissance can be realized?"

Why it matters: AI buildout economics now sit on top of energy availability, grid politics, and commodity price volatility. That matters for any AI-native financial product explaining markets to normal people.

Celsius Is the Still-Relevant Warning Label on Crypto-Banking

Fintech Business Weekly

The FTC ordered Celsius cofounders to pay a combined $16.1 million over deceptive claims tied to the 2022 collapse. Jason Mikula's deeper point is that Celsius looked like a crypto bank, promised bank-like safety and liquidity, and apparently lacked the basic internal systems to know what it owned and owed.

The timing matters because crypto and stablecoins are being pulled closer to mainstream financial infrastructure just as federal regulators are more receptive to new charters and novel banking models. My take: the lesson is less 'crypto bad' than 'financial products that mimic deposits need boring operational truthfulness before they get distribution.'

"The case for tokenization and the use of digital assets is strong but it is not a license to deceive."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro's trust problem: consumers need products that make risk legible instead of laundering risk through friendly UX and yield language.
