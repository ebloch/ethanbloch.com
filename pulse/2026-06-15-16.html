Abridge Wants to Become the Operating System of U.S. Healthcare

Crazy Stupid Tech

Fred Vogelstein's Abridge piece is the strongest signal today because it shows an AI-native company pushing beyond a wedge product into a full administrative platform. Abridge now says it serves more than 300 health systems, integrates with Epic, Oracle Health, and athenahealth, and reaches nearly 20% of primary care physicians and specialists serving roughly 250 million patients.

The interesting part is not just ambient scribing. Abridge is trying to own charting, billing, insurance workflows, research links, and patient records around the clinical conversation. That is a very Hiro-shaped lesson: the winning AI product may start as an assistant, but the real prize is becoming the workflow layer where fragmented incumbents and high-friction institutions have to meet.

My take: healthcare is a brutal market, but this is one of the clearest examples of "agentic vertical software" becoming infrastructure. The danger is Epic or Microsoft absorbing the interface; the opportunity is that trust, auditability, and workflow compression matter more than raw model access.

"I would think about this technology now like a team of assistants that can do all the different types of work that you wish you could get done."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro: Abridge is a case study in moving from AI feature to regulated workflow operating system, with trust and integration as the moat.

The Stock Market Is Getting Re-Equitized for the AI Buildout

Bloomberg Money Stuff

Matt Levine frames a regime change: after years when public markets mostly shrank through buybacks and private equity takeouts, AI infrastructure is forcing companies back to equity issuance. JPMorgan expects IPOs, secondaries, and other share sales to add about $1.5 trillion of stock to U.S. equity markets over the next two years, net of buybacks.

The underlying point is that AI changes the capital cycle. Hyperscalers, AI labs, infrastructure companies, power providers, and old companies retooling for AI all need capital at the same time. Profits and debt are no longer enough; equity becomes part of the financing stack again.

My take: this is the financial-market version of the same platform transition. If AI is a real industrial buildout, it does not just change products; it changes where capital comes from, how price discovery happens, and what public markets are for.

"Now investors are about to discover what happens when the supply suddenly comes rushing back."

Why it matters: Useful for Ethan's markets lens: AI may be turning public equities from a capital-return machine back into a capital-formation machine.
