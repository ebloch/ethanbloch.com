Avos: High-End Tax Rates May Be Near Secular Lows

Josh Blanchfield from Avos Capital

Josh Blanchfield argues that asset managers need to build for the tax regime that is likely coming, not the one that exists today. His historical setup is the 1920s: large cuts to top income, estate, and capital-gains taxes helped produce extreme inequality, then the Great Depression created the political conditions for sharply higher redistribution.

The modern rhyme is not subtle. The top 1% again controls a historically high share of income and wealth, cost-of-living pressure is feeding populism, and AI-related job losses could become another accelerant. For Ethan, the practical point is that investment performance is after-tax performance; strategies that look attractive under today's rules can become unusable if tax rates reset higher.

"Putting it all together, we think tax rates on the type of folks we work with are likely at secular lows, and unlikely to stay there for very long."

Why it matters: This is directly relevant to Hiro's advice layer: tax assumptions should not be treated as static plumbing. If a financial advisor is going to be genuinely forward-looking, it needs to reason about after-tax robustness across policy regimes, especially for affluent users.
