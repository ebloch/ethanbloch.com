San Francisco's June Primary Was a Clean Sweep for GrowSF's Slate

GrowSF

GrowSF's recap says Supervisors Stephen Sherrill and Alan Wong both won decisively with about 69% of the vote, while Prop A passed and Prop D failed. The broader signal: San Francisco's pro-governance, pragmatic reform coalition is still converting voter frustration into actual ballot outcomes.

The more interesting operational point is vote counting. Turnout appears to be roughly 270,000 ballots, higher than some low-turnout narratives and above GrowSF's own pre-election estimate, but the city still counted only about half the ballots on election night and then slowed sharply.

My take: for Ethan, this is local civic infrastructure, not just politics. If San Francisco wants to be credible on public-sector competence, election operations are an obvious place to demand modern service levels.

"Knowing the outcome of our elections—the most sacred aspect of democracy—is too important to delay."

Why it matters: This is useful Bay Area context: the reform bloc is winning, but execution details like election counting remain a governance bottleneck.

Quick Hits

  • GrowSF challenges the low-turnout narrative - The group says turnout is likely around 270,000 ballots, about 50,000 above June 2022. GrowSF
  • Why SF vote counting slows down - GrowSF says mail-in ballot counting pauses before election day so the department can prepare in-person voter rolls. GrowSF
  • GrowSF's proposed fix - Keep counting mail ballots, verify in-person voters in real time or treat in-person ballots as provisional, and update results hourly until done. GrowSF
