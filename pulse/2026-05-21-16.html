Agents Need Computers, Not Just Code Sandboxes

Latent.Space

The Daytona conversation is a useful marker for where agent infrastructure is moving: from disposable code execution boxes toward full, stateful, configurable computers that agents can reach through APIs. Ivan Burazin frames Daytona as the next version of the old 'end of localhost' thesis, except the user is now an agent rather than a human developer.

The most interesting numbers are operational, not philosophical: 60ms sandbox startup, 50,000 sandboxes in roughly 75 seconds, one customer running around 850,000 sandboxes a day, and RL/eval workloads moving from nothing to roughly half of usage in months. That is a pretty clear signal that agent systems are starting to need cloud primitives that look more like fast, stateful workbenches than simple serverless jobs.

My take: this maps directly to Hiro's product surface. If financial agents are going to reconcile accounts, inspect statements, file disputes, or safely manipulate external tools, they need controlled computers with state, auditability, and isolation. The sandbox market is becoming part of the agent trust stack.

"Agents today and going forward will need all these different compositions of computers to do different types of tasks."

Why it matters: For Hiro, the key point is that useful agents need durable environments, not just model calls. The infra layer is becoming a product constraint and a moat.

SpaceX's IPO Story Is Really Starlink Cash Flow Funding AI and Mars

On my Om

Om Malik reads the SpaceX prospectus as two stories stapled together: a spectacularly real Starlink broadband business and a much more speculative bundle of AI, xAI, Twitter, orbital data centers, and Mars-scale ambition. Starlink did $11.4 billion of revenue in 2025, $7.2 billion of adjusted EBITDA, and reached 10.3 million subscribers by March 31, 2026.

The hard question is whether the economics can carry a $1.75 trillion valuation. Starlink's margins look extraordinary, but ARPU has fallen from richer maritime and aviation customers toward lower-paying consumer growth. The V3 satellite and Starship story matters because it is the path to more capacity, better uploads, and potentially higher-value enterprise customers.

My take: SpaceX is a vivid version of the 2026 capital markets theme: real cash-flowing infrastructure is being asked to finance, justify, and narratively de-risk enormous AI ambition.

"Strip the prospectus to its financial skeleton and what remains is a satellite internet company."

Why it matters: This is useful market pattern recognition: durable distribution and infrastructure cash flows are being valued partly as AI option factories.
