Plan A Tries to Turn AI Doom Into a Governable Golden Path

Astral Codex Ten

Scott Alexander introduces the AI Futures Project's Plan A, a positive scenario for navigating frontier AI: US-China chip control, mutually audited data centers, a managed capabilities ceiling, broad access to powerful but controlled models, and a decade of top-human-genius AIs working on alignment before any true intelligence explosion.

The interesting move is that Plan A is framed less as a normal policy paper than as a benchmark for seriousness. If policymakers propose bans, acceleration, retraining, or marginal regulation, the challenge is whether they can describe an end-state at least as coherent as this one.

My take: the trustless US-China verification layer is the hard part, but the framing is useful for Ethan because it treats AI strategy as an institutional design problem rather than a vibe war between acceleration and pause.

"Plan A feels like our best hope to do something actually good."

Why it matters: Hiro lives in the downstream world of AI agents becoming economic infrastructure. This is a serious attempt to define the macro-regime those agents would operate inside.

Mythos-Class Models May Change the Logic of Chinese Hardware Risk

ChinaTalk

Mieke Eoyang argues that frontier models able to discover unknown vulnerabilities could shift supply-chain security from national-origin heuristics toward continuous, model-assisted auditing. If Mythos-class systems can rapidly find backdoors and bugs, the key question becomes whether a vendor transparently fixes discovered flaws, not simply whether the vendor is Chinese, American, or European.

That does not eliminate strategic supply-chain concerns. Jordan Schneider notes that many cyber justifications for bans also function as industrial policy, and physical dependence on Chinese drones, routers, batteries, or telecom equipment can matter even if software becomes more auditable.

My take: this is a useful inversion. AI may increase cyber offense in the short term, but it also makes opacity much harder to sustain as a product strategy.

"Once Mythos-class models are widely available, the question is not whether a product has a vulnerability, but what has it done to address the vulnerabilities that have been discovered."

Why it matters: For any financial AI product, trust will depend on verifiable operational security. This points toward AI-native assurance and audit workflows becoming product primitives.
