Bank Lobbyists Try To Pull Coinbase's Trust Charter Back Into the Fight

Fintech Business Weekly

The ICBA is asking the OCC to rescind or suspend Coinbase's conditional national trust bank charter, using New York's prediction-market lawsuit against a Coinbase subsidiary as the latest reason to question Coinbase's character, controls, and compliance posture. The broader issue is not just Coinbase: banks, consumer advocates, and Senator Warren are converging on an argument that crypto and stablecoin firms are trying to use trust charters to do banking-adjacent activities without full bank obligations.

The practical question is what these charters will actually permit. Custody is one thing; staking, payments, credit, Fed master account access, and direct integration into cash-flow products are where this becomes a banking-system fight. My take: this is the regulatory version of distribution capture. Crypto firms want the privileges and connectivity of bank rails; incumbents want to preserve the moat; the OCC is becoming the venue where the product roadmap gets negotiated.

"These companies are effectively crypto banks that want to evade the fundamental safeguards and obligations that come with being a bank."

Why it matters: Highly relevant to Hiro because AI financial advisors will live at the boundary between advice, money movement, custody, and regulated banking infrastructure. The charter question shapes what future fintech products can actually do.
