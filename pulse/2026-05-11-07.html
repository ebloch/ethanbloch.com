Agentic Inference Will Not Look Like Chatbot Inference

Stratechery

Ben Thompson's key distinction is between answer inference and agentic inference. Answer inference rewards token speed because a human is waiting. Agentic inference rewards memory, state, tool use, and cheaper capacity because the valuable work increasingly happens without a human in the loop.

That has real infrastructure implications: the current GPU stack remains dominant for training and fast answer generation, but long-running agents may shift demand toward memory hierarchy, CPU/tool-use speed, cheaper DRAM/SSD tiers, and systems that keep state around the model.

My take: this is directly relevant to Hiro/OpenAI finance agents. The hard product work is not just faster model calls; it is persistent context, reliable tools, state management, verification, and economics that make repeated background financial work cheap enough to run all the time.

"Agents need context, state, and history."

Why it matters: This sharpens the agent product thesis: durable financial agents need infrastructure around the model, not just model quality or raw latency.

Autonomous Systems as a Moral and Production Race

a16z

a16z argues that autonomous warfare should be framed less as distancing humans from violence and more as extending the American commitment to bring soldiers home. The piece ties values to engineering requirements: autonomy for evacuation, logistics, reconnaissance, and strike capability all come from the same technical stack.

The more operational point is production. The U.S. may have the AI and systems-quality advantage, but it is losing the manufacturing race for cheap attritable platforms. The author argues that autonomous systems need to be procured more like ammunition than exquisite aircraft.

My take: the dual-axis framing matters outside defense too. In AI products, quality without quantity is boutique capability; quantity without quality is noise. The winners will need both reliable intelligence and cheap enough deployment to make the system ubiquitous.

"We have the talent advantage, but we are losing the production race."

Why it matters: Useful strategic analogy for AI deployment: the bottleneck is often distribution, unit economics, and operational throughput, not just model capability.
