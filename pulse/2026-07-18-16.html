San Francisco cuts its housing tax to 5%, but leaves the Mission at 8%

GrowSF

San Francisco's Board of Supervisors voted 9-2 to lower the citywide inclusionary housing requirement from 15% to 5%, while exempting buildings with fewer than 24 homes. That is a real shift away from the 2016-era assumption that new housing can absorb large affordability mandates without killing project economics.

The caveat is the Mission carve-out: Supervisor Jackie Fielder won an amendment keeping the district at 8%, despite the Controller's warning that even above-zero requirements may keep projects from penciling. The policy story here is not just pro-housing momentum; it is how hard it remains for SF to let one clear economic rule apply evenly across neighborhoods.

My take: this is exactly the kind of local governance detail that matters for AI-era cities. Talent clusters need housing elasticity, and SF is still negotiating with itself over whether it wants abundance or symbolic affordability rules.

"requirements significantly above 0% would further threaten feasibility and would not create additional housing"

Why it matters: Useful local signal for Ethan: the city is moving toward buildability, but neighborhood carve-outs can still quietly erase the upside.
