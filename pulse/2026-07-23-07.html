Poolside Turns Model Building Into an Engineering Factory

Latent.Space

Poolside's Eiso Kant describes a model-building operation that runs 10,000 to 20,000 experiments a month with fewer than 70 researchers, compressing release cycles from six months to five or eight weeks. The interesting part is not just Laguna S 2.1 beating larger rivals; it is the operating system around training: streaming data, immutable datasets, reproducible experiments, low-precision compute, and agents that help launch, evaluate, and modify future training runs.

My take: this is the more durable AI story than any single benchmark win. If model quality starts to depend on factory throughput, verification loops, and research operations, the advantage shifts toward teams that can compound engineering process rather than just rent more GPUs. That maps directly onto agent products too: the harness and the feedback loop are becoming part of the model.

"model building is ultimately 90% engineering"

Why it matters: Useful frame for Hiro: agent quality will likely come from tight product-data-eval loops, not just choosing the smartest base model.

AI Debt Is Now Competing With Treasuries

Bloomberg Markets Daily

Bloomberg argues that the wave of long-maturity debt used to finance AI infrastructure is helping keep the 30-year Treasury yield above 5%. More than $500 billion of AI-linked financing is arriving at the same time as investors worry about US fiscal deterioration, leaving governments, hyperscalers, and non-hyperscalers all fighting for the same long-end capital.

My take: this is AI moving from software story to macro plumbing. The capex cycle is no longer just a question of Nvidia demand or cloud margins; it is starting to alter credit supply, rates, and the opportunity cost of capital across markets.

"credit is now competing with more borrowers on that long end for the same group of investors"

Why it matters: For a financial-advice product, this is the bridge between AI enthusiasm and household portfolio reality: higher long rates, equity duration risk, and infrastructure-financing spillovers.

China's Kimi K3 Lands Beside Xi's Open-Source AI Push

ChinaTalk

ChinaTalk treats Kimi K3 and Xi Jinping's World AI Conference speech as one combined signal: China wants to keep open models central to its AI strategy while using AI partnerships to court the Global South. The release appears strong enough to shake the US assumption that China is falling steadily behind on model capability.

The tension is strategic: open weights can expand Chinese influence and reduce dependence on US labs, but stronger models also raise governance questions inside China itself. If Beijing wants open source and control at the same time, the mechanism for catching dangerous capability before release becomes the hard part.

"Kimi and China may be closer to a Mythos model than we think."

Why it matters: This matters for any AI company betting on proprietary frontier access: open-weight capability and geopolitics can reshape cost, distribution, and competitive moats quickly.
