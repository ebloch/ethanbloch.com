Anthropic's Mythos Is a Branding Choice With Governance Consequences

On my Om

Om Malik argues that Anthropic naming its frontier model Mythos is not neutral branding. His frame: mythos is a received story, while logos is evidence and argument. For a company built around safety claims, that matters because the story can become a substitute for transparent evaluation.

The useful part for Ethan is not whether Om is being too sharp on Anthropic; it is the broader warning about AI companies using civilizational language to make power feel inevitable. My take: for an AI financial advisor, the opposite posture is more durable. Trust should come from legible process, auditability, and user outcomes, not priestly language about inevitability.

"Logos tells you how something works. Mythos tells you why it matters."

Why it matters: Good strategic reminder for Hiro: consumer finance trust is earned through explanation and accountability, not vibes around intelligence.

Tax-Aware Diversification Is Becoming the Real Wealth Product

Avos Capital

Josh Blanchfield uses the poker line 'money you don't lose still buys groceries' to argue that taxes are the biggest avoidable drag for many investors. The punchline: investors want more diversification precisely when global equity expected returns look weak, but the tax code often punishes the cleaner diversification path.

He points to demand for 351 exchange ETFs, long-short equity strategies, PPLI, and exchange funds as evidence that tax-advantaged diversification is moving from niche planning trick to mainstream product need. My take: this is the sort of pain an AI advisor should detect early, because the product opportunity sits at the intersection of portfolio construction, tax constraints, and behavioral fear of realizing gains.

"money you don't lose still buys groceries"

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro's wedge: better advice often means coordinating tax, diversification, and product selection rather than just optimizing an allocation.
