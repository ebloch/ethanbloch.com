Abridge Turns the Clinical Visit Into a Healthcare Intelligence Layer

Latent.Space

The Abridge interview is the clearest version of the vertical AI app thesis: start with a painful workflow, earn trust inside the system of record, then use the same context to expand across adjacent workflows. Abridge started with ambient clinical documentation, but the more important move is using the patient-clinician conversation as a shared substrate for doctors, patients, payers, pharma, prior authorization, and clinical decision support.

The scale numbers matter: 80M+ projected patient-clinician conversations this year across 250 health systems, 28+ languages, and 50+ specialties. That moves the company out of demo-land and into the constraints that decide whether AI apps become durable businesses: low latency, low cost, rigorous evals, domain experts in the loop, EHR integration, customer trust, and progressive rollout.

My take: this is directly relevant to personal finance. The equivalent question is not 'can an AI answer money questions?' It is whether the product captures the high-context moments where financial decisions are actually made, then turns that context into trusted downstream workflows.

"Context is king. Context is what puts them to work."

Why it matters: Abridge is a strong analog for Hiro/ChatGPT personal finance: vertical context, trust, workflow integration, and domain-specific evals are the moat, not just a better chat surface.

Ryan Cohen's eBay Bid Looks More Like an Activist Job Application

Bloomberg Money Stuff

Matt Levine reframes GameStop CEO Ryan Cohen's unsolicited eBay proposal as less of a real takeover and more of a public activist campaign to become eBay's CEO. Cohen does not appear to have the capital to buy eBay outright, so the 'bid' functions as a way to force shareholders and the board to evaluate his operating case.

The interesting part is how the meme-stock playbook is blending with old-school activism. Instead of only quiet board letters and proxy math, Cohen is using spectacle, media, and retail-investor attention as part of the campaign. That may look strange, but the underlying governance mechanism is familiar: argue that current management is misaligned, claim owner-operator credibility, and make shareholders compare plans.

My take: this is a useful example of attention becoming part of market structure. The stunt is not separate from the strategy; in a crowded information environment, it is one of the distribution channels for the strategy.

"It's eBay, it's the one."

Why it matters: Worth tracking as a governance and markets story: activist investing increasingly runs through social attention, not just institutional persuasion.
