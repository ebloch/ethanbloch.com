San Francisco's Slow Election Count Is an Operations Problem

GrowSF

GrowSF's special research report argues that San Francisco moved from near-immediate election-night clarity to multi-day or multi-week uncertainty because voting behavior changed faster than election operations. Nearly everyone votes by mail now, many ballots arrive in the final two days, and the Department of Elections still relies on manual signature review and a paper-roll process that pauses ballot counting around Election Day.

The sharpest point is causal: GrowSF estimates that the shift to mail voting explains only about 4 points of the roughly 15-point loss in election-night share from 2012 to 2024. The other 11 points come from how the city counts. That turns the story from a vague civic-trust complaint into a fixable process design problem.

My take: this is a useful local example of the broader AI-era lesson Ethan keeps circling: institutions can have the data and still be slow if their workflow is built around old bottlenecks. The proposed fixes are not exotic. They are e-pollbooks, better scanning flows, automated signature verification, and faster reporting cadence.

"The long count is a choice, and San Francisco can make a different one."

Why it matters: Relevant as local civic tech, but also as a clean case study in operational debt: the system did not fail because of one bad law or one bad tool, it failed because the workflow stopped matching user behavior.

Local Systems

  • Electronic Pollbooks Are the Biggest Lever

    GrowSF

    Replacing paper voter rolls with real-time electronic pollbooks would let San Francisco keep processing mail ballots instead of pausing for two days before and after Election Day.

  • Sign, Scan, and Go Targets the Polling-Place Ballot Pile

    GrowSF

    Placer County's model lets voters feed mail ballots into precinct scanners, cutting post-election processing by several days and addressing tens of thousands of ballots that otherwise sit in the canvass queue.

  • Automated Signature Verification Is a Compounding Fix

    GrowSF

    ASV would route high-confidence signature matches automatically and reserve human review for mismatches, with the reference set improving as new accepted signatures are folded back into the system.

Data & Accountability

  • A Public Dataset Back to 1849

    GrowSF

    GrowSF says it built the first public dataset combining San Francisco registration counts, in-person turnout, and absentee voting data back to 1849, creating a baseline for evaluating reforms.

  • County Comparisons Separate Voter Behavior from Operations

    GrowSF

    The county-by-county analysis attributes only part of San Francisco's slowdown to vote-by-mail adoption, with most of the decline explained by local counting practices.

  • The California Voter Foundation Reached Similar Recommendations

    GrowSF

    A separate California Voter Foundation study converged on similar ideas, including sign-and-scan, faster signature curing, voter education, and state funding for equipment, space, and staff.
