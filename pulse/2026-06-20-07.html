The AI Power Bottleneck Is Bigger Than Transformers

ChinaTalk

Dana Golden's ChinaTalk essay is the best thing in today's pile: it reframes the data-center power debate away from the familiar transformer shortage and toward the harder supply chain for power electronics, HVDC, solid-state transformers, SiC/GaN devices, gallium, and grid-control gear. The punchline is that even magic supplies of transformers and gas turbines would not make the grid ready for unconstrained AI buildout.

The useful analogy is chips: the strategic issue is not just capacity, but chokepoints, concentrated expertise, materials leverage, and industrial demand. My take: for Hiro and every AI-native business, power is becoming part of product strategy. The teams that understand energy interconnects, latency, compute cost, and jurisdictional risk together will see constraints before competitors do.

"The power grid supply chain is in many ways the AI supply chain."

Why it matters: This connects directly to AI infrastructure strategy: data centers are not only GPU procurement problems; they are grid, power electronics, and geopolitical supply-chain problems.

Open-Weight Coding Models Are Now a Real Strategic Alternative

AINews

GLM-5.2 dominated the AI chatter: practitioners are describing it as the first open-weight coding model they would seriously consider for workflows that previously required closed frontier systems. The coverage is careful about the caveat: this is not necessarily the best model overall, but it is now credible in frontier software-engineering range.

The practical implication is model independence. If open weights are good enough for large slices of coding, agents, and internal tooling, teams get more leverage on cost, deployment, fine-tuning, and vendor risk. My take: the model story is less important than the market structure story. Frontier access is becoming political and operationally fragile, so credible open alternatives matter even when they are slightly behind.

"The emerging consensus is not “best overall model,” but “open-weight model now credibly in frontier SWE range.”"

Why it matters: Hiro will need strong coding and financial-agent infrastructure over time; credible open models change the build-vs-buy and vendor-lock-in calculus.

Marriott Bonvoy Shows Loyalty Is a Payments Business in Disguise

FinTech Collective

FinTech Collective highlighted a sharp Marriott story: Bonvoy is expected to generate nearly $1 billion in co-branded card fees this year, while franchisees complain they absorb too much of the redemption cost when loyalty points turn into room nights. The hotel brand owns the customer and the card economics; the real-estate owners carry much of the operational burden.

This is a clean example of why distribution and wallet position beat asset ownership. My take: every consumer brand with repeat purchase behavior is trying to become a fintech, but the profit pool creates channel conflict when the brand, issuer, and operating asset are not the same party.

"The chain above the door has become a payments business in a bathrobe."

Why it matters: A useful fintech strategy reminder: loyalty, payments, and embedded finance can quietly become the highest-margin layer in an otherwise physical business.
