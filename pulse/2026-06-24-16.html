Databricks Wants to Be the Operating System for Enterprise Agents

Latent.Space

Matei Zaharia and Reynold Xin lay out Databricks' agent-era strategy: Omnigent as an open meta-harness above Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, internal agents, and custom tools; Lakebase and LTAP as the transactional/analytical data layer agents need to do real work. The strongest point is that agent quality in production may be less about frontier model deltas and more about context, permissions, state, spend controls, and live business data.

My take: this is directly relevant to Hiro. Financial advice agents will not win by being clever chat wrappers; they need governed access to accounts, transactions, documents, workflows, and user intent over time. Databricks is making the enterprise version of that argument from the infrastructure side.

"agents are only useful if they have the right context, permissions, memory, state, cost controls, and access to live business data"

Why it matters: Reinforces the core Hiro thesis: durable agent advantage comes from trusted data access, workflow integration, and controls, not just a better model prompt.
