The AI Long Trade Was Right, But the Funding Structure Was Wrong

Bloomberg

Matt Levine's lead on Situational Awareness is the cleanest version of today's AI-market lesson: you can be directionally right about the decade and still get wrecked by overnight funding. Leopold Aschenbrenner's fund rode the AI infrastructure thesis hard, reportedly reaching $45 billion before losses and margin pressure forced it to sell much of its public portfolio to Citadel.

The useful framing is not 'AI bubble' versus 'AI real.' It is duration matching. Data-center bonds and frontier-lab equity can wait years for the thesis to pay out; levered public-market books cannot. My take: this is the financial-market mirror of the product problem in AI agents. The underlying thesis may be right, but the operating constraints decide who survives long enough to harvest it.

"Situational Awareness is really really good at thinking about the long-term implications of AI, and Citadel is really really good at thinking about funding risk."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro's worldview: great long-term judgment still needs a financing and risk-control wrapper that survives volatility.

AI Persuasion Crosses the Human-Level Line, Then Immediately Gets Qualified

Astral Codex Ten

ACX flags a persuasion study claiming AI now beats expert humans, including champion debaters and professional canvassers, in a real-consequence donation setting. The caveat is important: when the models are forced to answer at human speeds and human message lengths, they fall back to human level.

This is the pattern worth noticing. A capability threshold gets crossed, then the discourse discounts it because the crossing happened in a narrow benchmark rather than the cinematic version people imagined. My take: for consumer finance, persuasion is not just a safety issue; it is a product design issue. Advice systems will need to prove they can guide without manipulating.

"now that that has been breached, we get to see how far it takes us."

Why it matters: Hiro lives in a persuasion-sensitive domain. The line between helpful coaching and undue influence will matter more as models get better.
