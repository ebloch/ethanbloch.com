SoftBank borrows against OpenAI to buy more OpenAI

Bloomberg

Matt Levine frames SoftBank's $10 billion loan against its OpenAI stake as the cleanest example of how AI equity valuations are being transformed into credit capacity. OpenAI and Anthropic may not carry much conventional debt themselves, but the data center and investor-financing ecosystem around them increasingly depends on underwriting their future cash flows.

The interesting part for Ethan is not the loan itself, but the reflexive loop: a private, loss-making AI company can indirectly support enormous amounts of debt because the market believes its equity is worth enough to absorb the risk. My take: this is the financialization layer of the AI boom becoming visible, and it matters for any AI company whose economics depend on infrastructure, enterprise confidence, and valuation staying mutually reinforcing.

"Borrowing against OpenAI to buy more OpenAI"

Why it matters: Useful lens for Hiro: AI valuations are becoming collateral, which will shape who can fund distribution, compute, and acquisitions.

Prediction markets may be a 100x better trading-fee product than stocks

Bloomberg

Levine's tick-size point is simple and sharp: prediction contracts pay $1 or $0, trade in one-cent increments, and often sit around 50 cents. That makes a penny spread roughly 2% of market value, versus about 0.01% for a typical stock trade.

Robinhood's numbers make the abstraction concrete: $156 million of transaction revenue from event contracts on 13.6 billion contracts, versus $129 million from nearly $1 trillion of stock volume. My take: the consumer-finance lesson is that new asset classes often win because the market structure is monetizable, not just because the narrative is hot.

"Robinhood's cut of event contract trading is about 100 times as big as its cut of stock trading."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to financial-product design: market structure and unit economics can matter as much as user demand.
