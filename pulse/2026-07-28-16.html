Codex Is Becoming the Agent Harness for Knowledge Work

Latent.Space

Latent.Space frames ChatGPT Work as the moment coding agents start breaking containment: Codex is no longer just a developer tool, because the same harness now powers work across documents, spreadsheets, slides, Sites, plugins, memory, and persistent agent environments.

The important product point is that knowledge work does not map neatly to one app. It is scattered across artifacts and context stores, so the winning interface may be an agentic workspace that assembles tools around an outcome instead of asking users to choose the right primitive first.

My take: this is unusually close to Hiro's product problem. Financial advice is also a cross-artifact workflow: accounts, documents, assumptions, plans, conversations, and actions. The model matters, but the durable advantage is increasingly the harness that can gather context, preserve state, and move cleanly between analysis and execution.

"There are roughly 100x more people who use code than who can write code."

Why it matters: This is directly relevant to building an AI financial advisor: the frontier is shifting from answer quality to persistent, tool-using work loops that non-experts can actually trust.

The Stock Market Is Not Your Rich Uncle

WSJ

Jason Zweig's warning is behavioral, not tactical: after a strong year for U.S. stocks, international stocks, emerging markets, and small caps, investors can start treating gains as house money. That is exactly when leveraged ETFs, prediction markets, thematic funds, and other flyers feel deceptively painless.

The useful framing is Peter Bernstein's: paper wealth exists because other investors are currently willing to pay more for what you own. That permission can disappear quickly, so the right response to a bull market is not necessarily to become more clever.

My take: this is the personal-finance version of AI evals. The visible number went up, but the underlying risk may have changed in ways the dashboard does not force you to feel.

"The stock market isn’t your rich uncle, and paper profits can go up in smoke at any moment."

Why it matters: Good raw material for Hiro's coaching voice: help users separate earned progress from lucky mark-to-market gains before they resize risk.
