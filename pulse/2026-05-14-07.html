Enterprise Moats Move From Systems of Record to Systems of Intelligence

a16z

a16z argues that CRM-style systems of record are starting to look like the old social graph: still valuable, but increasingly consumed as infrastructure by a higher-level feed. In enterprise GTM, that feed is a system of intelligence that researches accounts, coaches calls, writes back to the CRM, and turns the database into one input among many.

The Hiro relevance is direct: the durable product surface is less likely to be a prettier ledger and more likely to be the intelligence layer that turns financial records, goals, transactions, and policies into action. My take: the hard part is not just getting data access, it is becoming the trusted orchestration layer that users actually return to.

"The CRM isn’t going to go away, just like the friend graph never went away—but it’s turning into just an input."

Why it matters: This is almost exactly the fintech advisor thesis: financial accounts are the record, but the enduring product is the intelligence and action layer above them.

Coding-Agent Economics Shift From Subsidized Access to Metered Runtime

AINews

AINews frames Anthropic's new programmatic-usage policy as a normalization moment: Claude subscriptions now include separate API credits for third-party tools, while Anthropic keeps its most favorable economics inside Claude-owned surfaces. At the same time, OpenAI is using Codex enterprise incentives as a direct counterpunch, which makes developer harnesses feel less like side projects and more like strategic distribution channels.

The deeper point is that coding agents are moving from novelty into platform economics. Subsidies, limits, routing, enterprise security, and harness ownership are becoming competitive weapons, not footnotes.

"Both labs are doing very well, and these are in the grand scheme of things normal pricing shifts by people inventing the future of coding."

Why it matters: Useful operating signal for OpenAI and Hiro: agent UX matters, but runtime economics and distribution control may decide who can scale the workflow profitably.

Alphabet's AI Borrowing Spree Shows Capex Is Becoming a Credit-Market Story

Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports that Alphabet has borrowed nearly $60 billion in four months, selling debt across dollars, euros, pounds, francs, and yen to fund its AI buildout. Tech companies have already sold more than $300 billion of debt to US investors for AI spending, and bankers expect more issuers to tap overseas markets because domestic demand alone may not absorb the scale without pushing funding costs higher.

My take: this is the AI boom crossing from equity story to balance-sheet plumbing. If the next phase of AI depends on multi-year infrastructure bets, capital access and duration matching become part of the competitive stack.

"All this AI issuance makes you wonder at what point there are just too many bonds."

Why it matters: Important macro frame for AI strategy: compute scarcity is now also financing scarcity, and that can change who gets to keep building.
