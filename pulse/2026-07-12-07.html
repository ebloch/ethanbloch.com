Trump-Linked Fintech Tied to No-KYC Crypto Card Rails

Fintech Business Weekly

Jason Mikula traces a messy chain: World Liberty Financial invested in ALT5 Sigma, ALT5 bought MSwipe/Stradacarte, and Stradacarte allegedly enables corporate card programs where individual cardholder KYC can be auto-approved or avoided. The most relevant detail is not just the political connection; it is the bank-partner and program-manager stack that can obscure who is actually responsible for compliance.

For Hiro, this is a useful reminder that financial infrastructure risk often sits in the seams between sponsor banks, processors, program managers, and middleware vendors. The article also frames the bigger policy tension: stablecoin and crypto integration is moving faster just as enforcement intensity and reputation-risk supervision appear to be falling.

My take: this is exactly the kind of story that makes trustworthy consumer financial products harder to build. Users do not see the stack, but the weakest compliance layer shapes the whole category's reputation.

"The American financial system is in the midst of what one could charitably call a grand experiment."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro's trust surface: fintech credibility depends on hidden partner-bank controls, not just polished product UX.

Taiwan Deterrence Is Becoming a Gray-Zone Test

ChinaTalk

ChinaTalk's interview with Randy Schriver argues that Beijing is making sovereignty claims around Taiwan through routine activity: Coast Guard patrols, commercial-vessel radio queries, and rescues that physically normalize Chinese jurisdiction. Schriver's worry is that the U.S. can undercut deterrence not only by lacking munitions or logistics, but by sending quiet symbolic signals that Beijing reads as hesitation.

The strongest operational point is that Taiwan planning cannot focus only on invasion. A blockade or soft blockade may be more likely, and Schriver wants the U.S., Japan, the Philippines, and Taiwan to practice a Keelung-Yonaguni corridor to show that gray-zone pressure can be contested before it becomes war.

My take: this is a strategy piece hiding inside a defense interview. The durable idea is that alliances are not just resources; they are signaling systems.

"You would actually, unintentionally, make the invasion more likely if there's no pushback in the gray zone."

Why it matters: Ethan likes big systems problems. This one is about credibility, coordination, and incentives under uncertainty.
