AI Turns 10 Million Nazi Membership Cards Into a Warning About Fragile Systems

Josh Blanchfield from Avos Capital

Josh Blanchfield uses Memorial Day to make a systems point: durable institutions can feel inevitable right up until they are not. The hook is a Harvard/NBER project that used AI to digitize more than 10 million Nazi membership cards, expanding the dataset for historical study by roughly 200x.

The most useful takeaway is not a lazy present-day analogy; Blanchfield explicitly rejects that. It is that local social structures mattered. Town-by-town adoption appears to have been heavily influenced by community leaders, and many joiners were pulled by belonging, order, and national renewal rather than only explicit hatred.

My take: this is the constructive version of AI-for-archives. The model is not the story; the model unlocks a wider historical aperture, then humans have to do the harder work of institutional memory and judgment.

"If a thing is important to you - and it is fragile - it’s useful to know what causes it to break."

Why it matters: Ethan cares about institutions, incentives, and AI as a way to surface patterns humans could not practically inspect. This lands at that intersection.
