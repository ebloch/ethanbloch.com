The Next AI Goldrush Is Managing Token Workforces

a16z

a16z's essay reframes enterprise AI less as software automation and more as management science: every employee now has effectively unlimited agent headcount, but most organizations are turning that into loops, wasted context, and token spend that behaves like headcount bloat.

The sharpest point for Hiro is that durable advantage may live in evals and process encoding, not generic model access. Coding agents are working because code has native evals; financial-advice agents need the equivalent for advice quality, compliance, trust calibration, and successful user outcomes.

My take: this is the right lens. The next layer of value is not just better chat UX. It is converting messy, human financial workflows into repeatable judgment systems with tight feedback loops.

"A firm's eval suite will become its most valuable resource."

Why it matters: Directly relevant to Hiro: advice quality will come from proprietary process context, evals, and domain-specific loops, not simply picking the best frontier model.

The Monkey Supply Chain Is a Biotech Chokepoint

ChinaTalk

ChinaTalk traces an unglamorous but strategically important supply chain: research macaques. After China stopped exporting research monkeys in 2020, US vaccine and drug developers faced a severe shortage, with prices rising from a few thousand dollars to as high as $50,000.

The broader lesson is that frontier industries still depend on physical bottlenecks that do not scale like software. For advanced biologics, vaccines, gene therapy, and neurotechnology, alternatives are improving, but regulators and researchers still often need complete primate biology.

My take: this is a useful counterweight to AI abstraction. Strategic advantage often hides in slow, regulated, reputation-heavy supply chains where capacity takes years to build.

"At ChinaTalk, we write about supply chains quite often. This one is different because it is alive."

Why it matters: Good strategic pattern recognition: the bottleneck is not always compute. In regulated fields, trusted inputs, auditability, and institutional credibility can matter as much as raw technical capability.
