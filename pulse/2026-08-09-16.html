Japan's Yen Became America's Treasury Problem

Josh Blanchfield from Avos Capital

Josh Blanchfield uses Nixon's 1971 gold-standard break as the frame for today's dollar dependency: every country wants sovereignty, but none of them are actually self-sufficient. The concrete example is the U.S. Treasury reportedly helping support the yen because Japan is the largest foreign holder of Treasuries.

The interesting mechanism is simple but easy to miss: if the yen weakens hard, Japan may sell Treasuries to raise dollars and buy yen. That turns a foreign-exchange problem into a U.S. funding problem, because Washington needs foreign buyers to keep absorbing issuance.

My take: this is the cleanest macro reminder in today's batch that financial products are never local once balance sheets, currencies, and collateral chains are global. For Hiro, the user-facing implication is that 'why did my portfolio move?' increasingly needs cross-market explanation, not ticker-level narration.

"The Yen is their currency but became our problem."

Why it matters: Useful framing for Hiro's explanation layer: personal-finance advice has to translate macro plumbing into user-level consequences.

Microsoft's AI Capex May Be Lower Mostly on Paper

The Information

The Information argues Microsoft's apparently disciplined AI spending deserves a closer read. Microsoft is leaning more heavily on leased capacity from neoclouds and changed data-center useful-life assumptions, both of which can move costs away from the capex line investors are watching.

The strategic tension is that leasing let Microsoft move fast when OpenAI demand exploded, but it may also leave the company more exposed to third-party compute pricing over time. The story is less 'Microsoft is spending less' and more 'Microsoft has more flexibility in where the spend appears.'

"The spend is the same. It just shows up on a different line item."

Why it matters: Relevant to AI infrastructure economics and to reading public-company AI margins without taking headline capex at face value.
