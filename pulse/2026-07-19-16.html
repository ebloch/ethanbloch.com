The UK Is a Canary in the Coal Mine

Josh Blanchfield from Avos Capital

Josh Blanchfield argues that the UK is worth watching not because it is globally dominant, but because it shows an acute version of problems facing much of the developed world: persistent post-Covid deficits, weak productivity, poor business formation, and an aging corporate base.

The market signal is blunt: UK government bond yields are now the highest in the developed world, sitting around the same neighborhood as countries investors historically treat as much riskier borrowers. Avos has removed UK bonds from global strategic portfolios, not because collapse is imminent, but because the risk/reward has stopped clearing their bar.

My take: this is useful macro pattern recognition for Hiro. Personal finance advice increasingly needs to explain sovereign risk, inflation, and real yield dynamics in plain English, because households feel the second-order effects through mortgages, pensions, FX, and portfolio construction long before they understand the bond math.

"The UK currently has the highest government bond yields in the developed world, and pays slightly more to borrow than the Czech Republic and slightly less than Hungary."

Why it matters: Relevant to Hiro's advice layer: this is a clean example of how fiscal credibility, productivity, and capital-market pricing connect to household financial outcomes.
