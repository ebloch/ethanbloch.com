Vercel Treats Agents as a New Software Primitive

Latent Space / AINews

Andrew Qu's Vercel interview is useful because it moves past agent demos into the boring pieces that make agents shippable: provider fallbacks, resumable runs, skills, filesystems, subagents, observability, evaluations, and bot-readable product surfaces.

The practical framing is that agents are not just web apps with chat bolted on. They have different context, execution, and collaboration needs. Vercel's own path, from v0 pain points to the eve framework and Markdown responses for agent traffic, is a live example of a platform company rebuilding itself around agents rather than treating them as an add-on.

My take: this is directly relevant to Hiro. A financial advisor agent will need the same primitives, plus stricter trust, memory, permissioning, and review loops. The winning product surface may be less about a single chat UI and more about making the whole platform agent-friendly.

"I think agents are a new type of software. They are not as predictable as web applications."

Why it matters: This is the most concrete operating-system layer for consumer/workflow agents in today's batch: skills, resumability, files, and machine-readable interfaces.

AI Superforecasters Are Reaching Top-Human Territory

Astral Codex Ten

Scott Alexander reports that specialized forecasting scaffolds are now plausibly near the level of top human superforecasters, with anecdotes of bots making serious money on Kalshi and market-neutral portfolios. The important part is not the trading brag; it is that forecasting may become cheap, callable, and productized.

If AI forecasting becomes a normal tool, every strategic decision can get a probability estimate: product launches, hiring plans, market timing, regulatory outcomes, customer adoption, and investment theses. Forecasting also becomes an interesting way to let AI have bounded opinions, because the method is evaluated against future reality rather than brand politics.

My take: this is a major hint for Hiro. The most valuable financial AI may not just answer questions; it may force decisions into explicit forecasts, track calibration, and become a personal probability engine.

"Bots are now slightly below or equal to top humans. And bots improve at 0.9 Metaculus Elo points per month."

Why it matters: Financial advice needs judgment under uncertainty. AI superforecasting could become a core primitive for portfolio, planning, and life-decision advice.

Investors Are Watching Token Spend for the Next AI Trade Signal

Bloomberg Markets Daily

Bloomberg highlights the Silicon Data LLM Token Expenditure Index, which blends token price and usage to give investors a rough demand signal for AI. It nearly doubled from December, then fell almost 20% from a May high.

There are two readings. The bearish one is that AI companies are losing pricing power as customers get cost-sensitive. The bullish one is that cheaper tokens are expanding usage, so total spend doubling year over year is still evidence of real demand.

My take: token spend is a crude but important bridge between product usage and public-market narratives. For any AI business, the question is shifting from 'can it do the task?' to 'does usage expand faster than unit price collapses?'

"What can investors learn from a token?"

Why it matters: This is the market version of the AI product question Ethan lives with: whether falling inference cost creates margin compression or unlocks new demand.
