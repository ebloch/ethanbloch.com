Video Agents May Be the Next Turn in Generative Media

Latent.Space

Latent Space's interview with Ethan He frames the next leap in video not as a pure diffusion-model contest, but as an agent problem: systems that plan, generate, edit, critique, stitch, and iterate across a creative task. The analogy to coding agents is the useful part. Once one-shot model quality gets good enough, orchestration becomes the new surface area for differentiation.

The most interesting thread for Hiro is broader than video. Ethan argues that language-model intelligence increasingly supplies the leverage for other modalities, and later points toward models that understand their own context, memory, harness, and tool environment. That is very close to the question every serious agent product has to answer: when should capability live inside the model, and when should it live in the surrounding system?

My take: video agents are a specific case of a general pattern. Frontier products are becoming less about a single model call and more about durable, inspectable workflows where the model can manage tools, state, latency, cost, and user intent without losing the plot.

"In the near term, the next Sora won't be a better video model, but a video agent."

Why it matters: This maps directly onto agent architecture for finance: the winning system is likely the one that coordinates models, tools, memory, and deterministic checks better than the one with the flashiest single-step output.

Robinhood Opens the Door to Trading Agents, Carefully

Fintech Takes

Robinhood is letting users create separate accounts and wallets for AI agents, with access scoped to a pre-loaded balance. The agents can analyze portfolios, examine concentration and sector exposure, look through analyst notes, and execute stock trades through Robinhood's MCP service. The company is also introducing a virtual card designed for AI agents to make payments.

Alex Johnson's read is that this is unusually cautious for Robinhood: bring-your-own-agent, stock-only, and deliberately outside the core app experience. That caution may be rational. Full agentic trading collides with a business built around active retail engagement, where the user wants to trade, not delegate the fun away.

My take: this is exactly the shape regulated financial agents will probably take at first. Not omnipotent autonomy, but sandboxed authority, dedicated balances, explicit permissions, and narrow asset coverage. The product question is whether that feels empowering or neutered.

"The company didn't embed AI agents within its platform. It just built the integration layer allowing its customers to connect their own AI agents to their Robinhood accounts."

Why it matters: A practical benchmark for Hiro: the trust model may matter more than the intelligence demo. Scoped autonomy, auditability, and user control are the feature.
