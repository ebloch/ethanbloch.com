OpenAI's Reasoning Model Crosses From Benchmark Math Into Research Math

AINews

OpenAI says a general-purpose reasoning model disproved a long-standing belief around the planar unit distance problem, an Erdos problem dating to 1946. The important detail is not just that the model produced a valid result, but that the result was externally checked and treated by serious mathematicians as a real research contribution rather than a benchmark stunt.

The technical signal is long-horizon inference scaling. AINews notes that the reasoning summary ran to roughly 125 pages, and observers framed the result as evidence that test-time compute is now doing qualitatively different work. My take: for Ethan, this is the clearest version of the same pattern that matters in finance agents: the model is no longer only answering; it is searching, composing, and stress-testing over long chains where verification becomes the product boundary.

"the first really clear example of AI solving a well-known open math problem"

Why it matters: Hiro's hardest problems are not chat turns; they are long-running reasoning, tool use, and verification loops. This is a strong data point that those loops are becoming real product infrastructure.

Anthropic's SpaceX Compute Deal Turns AI Progress Into a Capital-Markets Story

TLDR AI

Anthropic reportedly agreed to pay SpaceX nearly $45 billion over three years for compute, including 300 megawatts from Colossus 1 and more capacity at a second SpaceX data center. The same newsletter flow says Anthropic revenue could more than double in Q2 to $10.9 billion and reach its first operating-profit quarter, even as future spending rises sharply.

This is the day's most explicit reminder that frontier AI is becoming a financing structure as much as a software market: revenue, power, data centers, IPO timing, and model competition are now one system. My take: the near-term winners may be the labs that can turn product pull into financeable infrastructure without having their margins eaten by cheaper models.

"It will pay $1.25 billion per month until May 2029"

Why it matters: For a financial advisor product, this matters because AI economics will shape pricing, reliability, and which model providers can be trusted as durable platform partners.

Parallel Wants to Price the Web for Agents

Stratechery

Ben Thompson interviewed Parallel founder Parag Agrawal about a search engine for AI agents that combines compute, models, and search, then prices results based on willingness to spend. The new Index by Parallel tries to estimate what content is worth to agents using Shapley Values, rewarding not just popular data but unique data that changes answer quality.

This is one of the more concrete attempts to solve the agent web's incentive problem. If agents become the dominant consumers of content, publishers need something better than robots.txt theater and lawsuits; they need attribution, price discovery, and a demand signal. My take: Parallel is attacking the right layer, even if the hardest part will be creating enough buyer liquidity for publishers to care.

"you combine information from them and you know the answer is B"

Why it matters: Hiro will depend on high-quality external knowledge. Agent-native content markets could determine whether assistants get reliable, legal, up-to-date information or a degraded web optimized against them.
