Thinking Machines Ships Inkling as an Open, Customizable Base Model

TLDR AI / AINews

Thinking Machines released Inkling, a 975B-parameter MoE model with 41B active parameters, native text/image/audio reasoning, and a one-million-token context window. The more interesting positioning is not benchmark maximalism; it is customization through Tinker, plus a smaller preview model meant to make the family usable at lower cost and latency.

For Ethan, this is another signal that the model market is splitting into frontier APIs, strong open/custom bases, and vertical fine-tunes. Hiro's durable edge probably will not come from picking one model forever; it will come from workflow data, evals, routing, memory, and product taste around the financial advice loop.

My take: Inkling matters less as a single model launch than as a proof point that serious American open-weight competition is moving back into the game.

"Our model, called Inkling, is a Mixture-of-Experts transformer with 975B total parameters, 41B active."

Why it matters: Open/customizable models lower the cost of specialized agent behavior, which is directly relevant to financial advice agents that need domain adaptation and long-context personalization.

The Lab of the Future Looks Like an AI Data Center

Latent.Space

Latent Space's Lila Sciences episode frames scientific discovery as the next internet-scale data frontier: an automated wet lab becomes a token generator, nature becomes the verifier, and robotics turns experiments into an always-on data pipeline. Lila says it has built more than 10 trillion experimentally verified scientific reasoning tokens.

The interesting business idea is that the bottleneck shifts from model architecture to data production infrastructure. In software and finance, the analogous question is: what proprietary feedback loop can produce verified reasoning traces at scale?

My take: the best AI companies increasingly look like data factories with unusual actuators. For science, that actuator is a lab; for Hiro, it may be bank connections, plan outcomes, user intent, and advisor-grade review.

"The lab as a data center. Instruments as nodes on a graph, a magnetically levitating "PCI bus" transport layer between them, orchestration as a slurm queue."

Why it matters: A useful analogy for Hiro: the winning loop is not just model access, but verified domain data generated by the product itself.

Stripe and Advent Reportedly Bid for PayPal

TLDR Fintech

Stripe and Advent reportedly offered $60.50 per share for PayPal, valuing the company above $53 billion, with roughly $50 billion in committed bank financing. The deal logic is clear: payments scale, checkout distribution, Venmo, cross-border reach, crypto rails, and AI-driven operating leverage all matter more as payments becomes infrastructure for agentic commerce.

The same TLDR issue also points to Visa packaging an AI financial assistant for banking apps. Incumbents are not waiting for startups to own the consumer financial interface.

My take: if this bid is real, it is a blunt reminder that distribution and trust still matter in fintech. AI changes the UX, but balance-sheet scale, acceptance networks, and regulatory posture remain hard assets.

"The proposal would give Stripe and Advent equal ownership of PayPal and reflects accelerating payments M&A as firms seek scale, AI-driven efficiencies, and stronger positions in checkout, Venmo, crypto, and cross-border payments."

Why it matters: Payments consolidation and bank-distributed AI assistants both shape the competitive map for an AI financial advisor.
